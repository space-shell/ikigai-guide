# Phase 1 — Synthesis: frameworks, agreements, disagreements

## 1. What the word actually means (etymology)

- **iki** (生き) — from *ikiru*, to live; relates to **daily living**.
- **gai** (甲斐) — worth/value; traces to *kai*, shell — shells were valuable
  in the Heian period (794–1185) and used in the aristocratic Kaiawase game
  ([Kemp, 2020](https://medium.com/ikigai-insights/ikigai-is-not-a-venn-diagram-cca7abba323)).
- So: "the worth/value found in daily living" — not "grand life purpose".
  Kamiya (1966) notes the word has no equivalent in English/German/French;
  translators fall back on "worth living" / "meaning to live"
  ([translation quoted at Ikigai Tribe](https://ikigaitribe.com/blogpost/ikigai-ni-tsuite/)).

## 2. The frameworks found in the material

| Framework | Source | Core structure |
|---|---|---|
| Sources vs feeling | Kamiya 1966 | **ikigai** (sources: people, hobbies, work, memories) + **ikigai-kan** (the felt sense life is worth living) |
| Seven needs (+ purpose) | Kamiya 1966; Kemp 2022 | Life satisfaction · change & growth · bright future · resonance · freedom · self-actualization · meaning & value · (sense of mission) |
| Five pillars | Mogi 2017 | Start small · release yourself · harmony & sustainability · joy of little things · here and now |
| Two-axis map | Mogi (video, via [Lee Byron](https://leebyron.com/til/ikigai/)) | Axes small↔big, private↔public; *everything on the map is ikigai*, not a centre point |
| Four-circle Venn | Zuzunaga 2011–12 → [Winn 2014](https://theviewinside.me/what-is-your-ikigai/) | what you love / are good at / world needs / can be paid for; ikigai at the intersection |
| Pleasure + mission | Tamashiro 2019 | two-word ikigai ("to delight"); enemies-of-ikigai fears |
| Everyday practice | Mitsuhashi 2018 | small daily joys; past-sifting; job crafting; action |
| Logotherapy / Morita | Frankl; Morita — via García & Miralles ch. 3–4 | meaning found under all circumstances; act *before* motivation; journaling |
| Measurement | Ikigai-9 (Imai 2012; Fido 2019) | 3 subscales: emotional well-being · growth/future orientation · contribution/meaning |

## 3. Where they disagree — and what to make of it

### 3.1 The Venn diagram is not Japanese (settled, and admitted)
Documented genealogy, verified from primary accounts:
1. Zuzunaga, a Spanish astrologer, draws a four-circle **purpose-of-work**
   diagram (c. 2011–2012), first published in Borja Vilaseca's book
   *¿Qué harías si no tuvieras miedo?* — his own
   [account on the Ikigai Podcast](https://ikigaitribe.com/ikigai/podcast07/).
2. Marc Winn, having watched Dan Buettner's
   [TED talk](https://www.ted.com/talks/dan_buettner_how_to_live_to_be_100)
   on Okinawan centenarians, pastes "ikigai" into the diagram's centre
   ([his post, 14 May 2014](https://theviewinside.me/what-is-your-ikigai/)).
   Winn now explicitly credits Zuzunaga ("I added a single word to a shape
   that already existed").
3. The meme travels; Zuzunaga — who "lacks in-depth knowledge" of ikigai —
   watches his astrology-derived diagram become *the* Western symbol of a
   Japanese concept.

Commenters on Winn's post trace the same shape to earlier purpose diagrams
(Jim Collins' hedgehog concept, a 2010 Corey Mandell post) — the Venn is a
Western career-counseling composite that predates its Japanese branding.

**Implication for the site:** we cannot honestly *teach* the Venn as ikigai.
We can *use* it — clearly labelled as a Western adaptation, as one optional
lens — because it remains a genuinely useful career/fulfilment model
([Lee Byron](https://leebyron.com/til/ikigai/): "I still really like this
mental model" despite the misappropriation).

### 3.2 Does "what you can be paid for" belong in ikigai at all?
- **No, per the Japanese conception.** The money circle comes from Zuzunaga's
  *purpose-of-work* diagram, not from Japanese usage. Kemp: ikigai need not
  involve work or money at all — a grandchild, a morning coffee, a fishing
  hobby are textbook ikigai. Mitsuhashi: treating ikigai as a career question
  is the classic Western mistake. Kamiya's sources-of-ikigai are mostly
  non-commercial. Japanese has no word for *retirement* — ikigai often
  *outlasts* paid work.
- **Pragmatically, yes for many users.** A majority of Western searchers
  arrive with a career question. The Venn's "paid for" circle answers a real
  need (fulfilling livelihood) — it's just not ikigai proper. García &
  Miralles themselves note ikigai often *is* one's work in Japan, and Kemp's
  eighth need (sense of mission) shades into vocation.
- **Resolution for the site:** the journey proper treats ikigai as
  work-optional and plural; a clearly-marked **optional "livelihood lens"**
  stage applies the four-circle model *on top of* the user's discovered
  sources for those who want to connect ikigai to earning. This respects both
  the evidence and the audience.

### 3.3 One centre vs many small sources
The Venn implies a single answer hidden at the intersection — a puzzle to
solve. Kamiya/Mogi/Mitsuhashi describe **plural, revisable, small** sources:
multiple ikigai coexisting, growing and fading across a life; Mogi's map puts
ikigai *everywhere* on the diagram. An intersection-centred UI actively
teaches the wrong model. The site should scaffold *collecting many sources*
and *strengthening the feeling*, not "finding The One".

### 3.4 Feeling vs achievement
Western material frames ikigai as a thing you obtain; Kamiya defines
ikigai-kan as a **present-tense feeling** with "a clearer attitude toward the
future" (Inoue, via Kemp) — available on the way to goals, not only at their
completion. Consequence: exercises should target *experiencing* (little
things, presence, resonance) as much as *discovering*.

### 3.5 Purpose vs ikigai
Kamiya keeps *shimei-kan* (sense of mission) **distinct from** ikigai-kan:
purpose correlates with ikigai but is one ingredient, not the whole. Kemp
elevates it to an eighth need. The Venn flattens the distinction ("purpose"
sits at the centre). The site should treat purpose as a *possible outcome*
for some users, not the definition for all.

### 3.6 The longevity story
García & Miralles/Buettner sell ikigai as "the Japanese secret to a long
life". The cohort evidence is real —
[Sone 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18596247/),
[Tanno 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19539820/),
[Mori 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2017.02.013),
[Okuzono 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanwpc/article/PIIS2666-6065(22)00010-4/fulltext) —
but it is observational association, and the studies measure ikigai as a
simple felt sense, *not* the Venn's constructs. Kemp: "ikigai is not from
Okinawa, and not a longevity secret." Honest framing: meaningful living is
associated with better health outcomes; it is not a pill.

### 3.7 Where everyone agrees (the recurring threads)
These recur across every serious source, and form the exercise vocabulary:
1. **Start small** — Mogi's first pillar; Mitsuhashi's daily joys; Kamiya's
   mundane sources.
2. **Plural sources** — never one answer.
3. **Feeling is the ground truth** — "the most honest aspect of our ikigai
   are our feelings" (Kamiya).
4. **Needs can be deliberately satisfied** — the 8 needs are a checklist for
   designing a life, not a personality quiz.
5. **Action before insight** — Morita therapy; Mitsuhashi's "ikigai is the
   action we take"; experiments beat introspection.
6. **Contribution/resonance** — every framework includes a relational or
   contribution dimension (Ikigai-9 subscale 3; Venn's "world needs"; Mogi's
   harmony pillar).
7. **Attention/presence** — here-and-now pillar; joy of little things; flow.
8. **Movement forward** — ikigai-kan has "a clearer attitude toward the
   future"; change & growth is a named need.
9. **It changes across a lifetime** — sources fade and are replaced; the
   process is cyclical, not one-shot.

## 4. Conclusion

The four-circle Venn and the original Japanese conception answer **different
questions**: "what career should I have?" vs "what makes daily life feel worth
living?". The deep, validated material (Kamiya's needs, Ikigai-9, the cohort
studies, the pillars) is almost entirely locked in prose; the interactive
material is almost entirely shallow Venn form-filling. A site that combines
the authentic model with real interactivity has no existing counterpart —
see [gap analysis](gap-analysis.md).
